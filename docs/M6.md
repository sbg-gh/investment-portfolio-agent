# M6 paper portfolio simulation: separate ledger, reproducible fills, FIFO lots and the isolation proof
## What M6 is, and is not
M6 is a simulation. A user can open a paper account against one of their portfolios, deposit synthetic cash, place buy and sell orders, and read positions, valuation and a reconciliation. Every fill is a deterministic function of recorded inputs, so the same inputs always give the same fill.
It is a deterministic correctness gate, not a performance claim. Nothing here reports a return, backtests a strategy or implies a paper result would have been achieved in a market. There is no live mode, no broker credential, no execution adapter and no network or HTTP code in the paper module. The eighth eval suite checks correctness only.
## Isolation design
The central property is that paper activity can never change real holdings. It is enforced at the schema level and then proven by tests.
- Schema. `paper_accounts` and `paper_ledger` have foreign keys only to `users`, `portfolios`, `securities`, `market_observations` and `paper_accounts`. No column, key or trigger references `holdings`, `transactions`, `import_receipts`, `holding_events` or `paper_orders`.
- No write path. `paper.py` writes only the two paper tables. It never reads or writes the real-state tables or `baseline_coverage`. A structural test scans its imports and SQL.
- Byte-identical test. Real state is built (imports, transactions, a coverage row, a snapshot, an M0 intent). Ordered full-row dumps are hashed, broad paper activity runs for two users, and every dump is identical afterwards.
- Authorizer proof. The paper workload runs on a fresh SQLite connection whose authorizer denies every read and write of the real-state tables. It succeeds, and a control shows the authorizer is live.
- Converse. No opening positions are copied in either direction. Paper positions come only from paper buys. A new paper account on a portfolio that holds X has no X, and selling X is rejected. Real transactions do not change paper cash or positions, and baseline coverage is unaffected.
## Accounts and ledger
A paper account has a portfolio, a USD currency, a price source and a fee model. These are immutable after creation, which keeps fills reproducible. The ledger is append-only (update and delete triggers abort). Each entry is a deposit, buy or sell with a Decimal cash delta.
Entries are idempotent on `(account, idempotency_key)` with the M2 replay semantics: a retry returns the prior entry with `replayed` true and is not re-evaluated, even if conditions changed. The same key in another account is independent. Cash and positions are always derived from the ledger, never stored.
## Orders and rejections
`submit_order` checks in this order: account authorization, mode, side, quantity, idempotency key, cutoff time, idempotent replay, security existence and kind, observation selection, freshness, then cash and position.
Rejections are `insufficient_cash`, `insufficient_position`, `price_missing`, `price_stale`, `nav_missing` and `nav_stale`. A rejection raises `PaperRejected` and writes nothing. The service layer records only the payload-free `invalid` trace.
There is no pending state. A pending order would need a scheduler and would make the result depend on when data arrives. Retry with a NEW idempotency key once data is available. A retry under the same key after a rejection re-evaluates, because nothing was stored.
## Stock and ETF fills
A fill uses the latest observation from the account source with `observed_at` and `fetched_at` both at or before the cutoff. It calls the same `select_observation` function as valuation, so the selection rule is structurally shared. An observation older than the freshness window (price one day, optional policy override) is rejected as stale, and one exactly at the limit fills.
The fill price is the observation value. There is no slippage and no claim about intraday execution. Fees are explicit and exact, never rounded:
- `none`: zero.
- `flat`: the amount per order.
- `per_share`: the amount times quantity.
- `percentage`: a fraction of gross (0.001 is 0.1%).
A buy debits gross plus fee and a sell credits gross minus fee. A buy that would take cash below zero is rejected, as is a sell whose fee exceeds its proceeds at zero cash.
## Fund NAV semantics and the fill-date assumption
A mutual fund fills only from a `nav` observation and never from a price. A price observation cannot even be ingested for a fund. The fill uses the latest published NAV with `observed_at` and `fetched_at` at or before the cutoff and records its `nav_date`. NAV freshness is three days.
This is a documented assumption. Forward pricing and the 4pm cutoff are NOT modeled: the order fills at an already published NAV, not the next one it would receive in practice. A NAV is never presented as an intraday quote.
## FIFO lots and cost basis
Buys create lots and sells consume the oldest lots first. The fee is capitalized into the buy basis (quantity times price plus fee) and a sell fee reduces proceeds. For a partially consumed lot the consumed basis is remaining basis times consumed over remaining quantity, rounded to 12 places half-even. A fully consumed lot uses exactly its remaining basis, so consumed plus remaining always equals the original with no residue.
Realized P&L is proceeds net of fee minus consumed basis. It is accounting, not performance. All arithmetic uses Decimal in a fixed 80-digit context, never float.
## Corporate actions: deferred
Splits, dividends and mergers are not modeled. The ledger has no entry types for them and positions do not adjust. A user comparing a long paper history against a real split would see a mismatch. This is a documented limitation and a later milestone.
## Relationship to M0 `paper_orders`
The M0 `paper_orders` table and `record_paper_order` are untouched. They remain a reference-price intent annotation with no cash, lots or fills, and are still mode-gated. The paper ledger neither reads nor writes them. No migration is needed because the two features share no data; replacing the M0 table would break the M5b harness and its tests for no benefit.
## Mode gate
Mutators (create account, deposit, order) accept only the exact string `paper` (`type(mode) is str`). The default is `read_only`. `live`, `LIVE`, empty, `None`, `paper ` with a space and any unknown value are rejected with a generic `PermissionError`. There is no broker credential, adapter, network call or HTTP code. Reads (ledger, positions, valuation, reconcile) need authorization but not paper mode, and write only a trace.
## Valuation and reconciliation
`value_paper_account` values cash plus open positions with the same stale and missing semantics as portfolio valuation. A stale or missing price withholds the total (`total` is null) and reports the priced subtotal; nothing is substituted or fabricated, and no snapshot row is written. Valuation uses the current ledger with the market cutoff only.
`reconcile_paper_account` recomputes cash from deposits and fill components and compares it with the summed cash deltas, and compares per-security signed quantities with open-lot quantities. It flags divergence and never repairs or coerces it.
## Schema upgrade v8 to v9
The migration runs in an immediate transaction with a foreign-key check, rejects a future version, and alters no existing table (`paper_orders` is byte-identical). A deliberately broken v9 upgrade leaves `user_version` 8 with no partial paper tables, and reopening the same file then reaches v9. The migration tests cover rollback and recovery.
## Backup and restore
Backup and restore carry the paper tables forward at v9. The foreign-key check is empty after restore, all credentials are revoked, and the append-only triggers still hold.
## Services and privacy
Seven `PortfolioService` methods wrap the module through `_run`: create account, deposit, submit order, ledger, positions, valuation and reconcile. None accepts a user or owner argument, so a caller-supplied owner id is a `TypeError`. Cross-user, forged, expired, revoked and malformed-scope calls are denied with nothing written. Traces keep the M1 columns and carry no account ids, amounts or symbols. The chat tool allow-list has no paper tool.
## Evaluation suite
`m6-paper-baseline-v1` is the EIGHTH suite in `cli eval`: 17 cases in `m6_cases.json` (`m6-001`..`m6-017`). Each runs an isolated in-memory store through the authorized services with synthetic securities and fixed datetimes. Scenarios cover untouched real holdings, rejected non-paper modes, overdraft, oversell, reproducible fills, post-cutoff exclusion, stale and missing price, dated NAV, stale NAV, exact fees, FIFO basis, reconciliation, tamper detection, idempotent retry, cross-user denial and withheld totals.
It is fail-closed: mutating an expected answer drives `passed` to 0, changes the dataset hash and makes `cli eval` exit nonzero. The report has no `learning_improvement` or `held_out_evaluation` key. The recorded baseline is `evals/baselines/m6-paper-v1.json`, and a test requires the current report to equal it.
## TDD evidence
Each behavior was driven red first. The v9 migration tests failed on `user_version` (8 != 9) and the missing paper tables; the ledger, fill, lot and valuation tests failed on the missing `paper` module; the service tests failed with `AttributeError` for `create_paper_account`; the eval tests failed with `ModuleNotFoundError` for `m6_evaluation` and the CLI test with 7 reports instead of 8. After implementation the full suite passed (519 tests). `docs/TESTING.md` has the counts and gate commands.
## Limitations
- Corporate actions, tax-lot selection other than FIFO, tax reporting and wash-sale logic are not modeled.
- Fills have no slippage, partial fills, spreads or market impact, and no intraday execution is claimed.
- Fund fills use an already published NAV, not forward pricing.
- Currency is USD only, and there are no margin, short or options positions.
- Prices come only from operator-ingested observations; no real market-data adapter exists.
- A paper result is a simulation and says nothing about real returns.
